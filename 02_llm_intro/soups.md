**1. Tom Yum Goong (Thai Shrimp Soup)**  
A hot-and-sour Thai classic celebrated for its bright, aromatic broth infused with lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime, and fresh chilies, typically loaded with succulent shrimp and mushrooms.  
- Shrimp (peeled, de-veined)  
- Lemongrass (finely bruised or minced)  
- Galangal (sliced into rounds)  
- Kaffir lime leaves (torn)  
- Lime juice (fresh)  
- Fish sauce  
- Thai bird’s eye chilies (whole or sliced)  
- Oyster or straw mushrooms (quartered)  
- Cilantro stems (for cooking) and leaves (for garnish)  
- Water or chicken stock  

**Steps:**  
1. Bring water or stock to a gentle boil with lemongrass, galangal, and kaffir lime leaves; simmer 5 minutes to extract flavor.  
2. Add shrimp and mushrooms, cooking just until the shrimp turns pink and opaque (about 3–4 minutes).  
3. Stir in lime juice and fish sauce, adjusting saltiness and acidity to taste.  
4. Add chopped chilies and cilantro leaves, then serve immediately in bowls with steamed jasmine rice.

**2. Miso Soup (Japanese)**  
A staple of Japanese cuisine, this light, umami-packed soup relies on a delicate dashi base and fermented miso paste, often enriched with silky tofu and briny nori.  
- Dashi stock (premade or made from kombu kelp and bonito flakes)  
- White miso paste (shiro miso) or red miso (aka miso)  
- Firm tofu (cubed)  
- Nori strips (dried seaweed)  
- Green onions (sliced thin)  
- Optional: soy sauce, sesame oil, or sliced shiitake mushrooms  

**Steps:**  
1. Gently heat dashi in a pot; avoid boiling once miso is added to preserve probiotics and flavor.  
2. In a small ladle, scoop a spoonful of miso and whisk with a little hot broth until smooth, then stir the mixture back into the pot.  
3. Add tofu cubes and torn nori, heating just until warmed through (about 2 minutes).  
4. Garnish with sliced green onions and serve immediately in small bowls, ideally with steamed rice.

**3. Pho (Vietnamese Noodle Soup)**  
Vietnam’s iconic aromatic soup features a deeply flavored, clear broth simmered for hours with charred aromatics and warm spices, served over rice noodles and customized with fresh herbs and condiments.  
- Beef bones (marrow and knuckle) or whole chicken  
- Charred onion and ginger (halved and blackened over flame)  
- Spices: star anise, cinnamon stick, cloves, coriander seeds, fennel seeds  
- Fish sauce (nuoc mam)  
- Rock sugar  
- Rice noodles (banh pho, dried or fresh)  
- Thinly sliced raw beef (or poached chicken)  
- Bean sprouts, Thai basil, cilantro, lime wedges  
- Condiments: hoisin sauce, sriracha, chili garlic sauce  

**Steps:**  
1. Roast onion and ginger until charred, then combine with bones, spices, fish sauce, and rock sugar in a large pot; cover with water and simmer gently for 6–8 hours, skimming impurities regularly.  
2. Strain the broth, discard spices, and keep warm; adjust seasoning with extra fish sauce or sugar if needed.  
3. Cook rice noodles according to package directions, drain, and divide among serving bowls.  
4. Ladle hot broth over noodles, top with raw beef slices (they’ll cook in the hot broth) or poached chicken, then arrange bean sprouts, herbs, and lime wedges on the side; let diners customize with hoisin and sriracha to taste.
